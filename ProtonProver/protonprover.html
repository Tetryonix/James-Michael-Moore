Read this in order: the STL outer bound is a geometric maximum, the purple scan measures empty gap, and the green scan measures center-to-facet distance. The experimental proton RMS charge radius is a different definition—not a hard outer wall.
  1. R = center → STL outer sphere.
  2. G = mean purple sphere → nearest-triangle gap.
  3. S = mean green center → triangle-interior distance.
  4. Compare C = R − G with S. This comparison is exact arithmetic, but C is not a new measured radius because purple lines may point sideways.

Why A and S cannot be turned into one unique “true radius”

A averages the outer silhouette. For every viewing direction it deliberately selects the farthest projected vertex, so it is an outer-size statistic. Interior points play no part in that maximum.

S averages distances from the center to points sampled across all 20 regular exterior triangle faces. It weights triangle area rather than viewing direction, so it remains distinct from A.

A midpoint such as (A + S) / 2 can always be computed, but it is only an arithmetic blend. Geometry supplies no law saying the two statistics should receive equal weight, so calling that midpoint the true proton radius would add an unsupported assumption.

The collapsed center-out radial check answers a third, explicit question: along each direction, stop at the first triangle in the way, or reach the outer sphere if nothing blocks the ray. It is useful for understanding this model, but it is still not the experimentally defined RMS charge radius.
